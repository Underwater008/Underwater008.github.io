02 · DISCLOSURE

DisclaimerThis is not a recycled project.

This disclosure is here to be explicit: this project is new, not reused from an older class project.

Anthony Alvarez had already printed physical Masterworks of Horror cards before I touched any of this — but as marketing samples, not a finished product. What I made is also for marketing, but I have higher standards for presentation and visual appearance.

But those cards were what they were — not foil, not in blister packs, the cuts uneven, a QR code stamped on the front of every card. Functional. Not "shelf."

Everything you'll see from here on — the foil layers, the AI mask pipeline, the supplier negotiation, the blister packs, the print run, the box on the desk — did not exist before this project. If this project hadn't happened, none of it would have been made.

This is a brand-new artifact, built specifically as my response to The Pleasures of Book Collecting.

06 · FOIL MASKS

How a foil card is actually made.

The base of a foil card is a sheet of holographic foil. The printer then lays down white ink wherever the foil should NOT show through, then prints the colored art on top.

So every card needs three files — the same three the supplier asks for in Chinese:

原图 · Original — the original art (full color)
不闪图 · Non-foil mask — black where ink covers foil, white where foil shows through
闪图 · Foil front — the final front face with selective foil

Designing a hundred of these by hand was not an option.

07 · AUTOMATION

So I built an AI pipeline to generate the masks.

Each of Anthony's 70+ source PNGs went through a node-based workflow that isolated the figure, posterized to black/white, and exported the mask as a print-ready 300dpi JPG named to the supplier's convention.

07 · OVERLAY QA

Then I eyeballed every mask in one pass.

Masks dropped back over originals in red — the whole set on one page. Anything wrong got hand-fixed before shipping.

13 · CLOSING

And then we sorted 1,500 collab cards by hand.

We ordered 5,000 cards. The factory shipped us more than that. 1,500 of them were collab cards we'd done with other games at USC — those came loose, not in packs, so we had to sort them by hand.

The Pleasures of Book Collecting argues that what makes an object collectible is the care that went into making it sit on a shelf. I made the cards collectible — the foil, the bag, and the box on the desk.

That's the response.
